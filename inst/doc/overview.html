Overview of BayesianQDM

Decisions in a proof-of-concept study

BayesianQDM implements the Bayesian quantitative decision-making framework of Kang, Yamaguchi and Han (2026) for proof-of-concept (PoC) studies. The data of a PoC study lead to one of three decisions: Go (proceed to the next phase of development), NoGo (stop the development) or Gray (the evidence is not sufficient for either decision).

Let \(\theta\) be the treatment effect, the difference between the treatment and control groups in the response rate (binary endpoint), the mean (continuous endpoint) or the survival probability at a milestone time (time-to-event endpoint), or the ratio of the hazards of the two groups (time-to-event endpoint). The decision criteria use two values of the effect chosen by the study team: the target value \(\theta_{TV}\), an effect worth pursuing, and the minimum acceptable value \(\theta_{MAV} < \theta_{TV}\), below which the treatment is not of interest. Given the PoC data \(D\), the criteria based on the posterior probability are

  • Go criterion: \(\Pr(\theta > \theta_{TV} \mid D) \ge \gamma_{go}\),
  • NoGo criterion: \(\Pr(\theta \le \theta_{MAV} \mid D) \ge \gamma_{nogo}\).

The decision is Go if only the Go criterion is met, NoGo if only the NoGo criterion is met, and Gray if neither is met. If both are met, which for one endpoint is possible only when \(\gamma_{go} + \gamma_{nogo} \le 1\), the decision is called Miss; the package reports Miss decisions separately and warns when they have positive probability.

Kang et al. (2026) write the NoGo criterion as \(\Pr(\theta > \theta_{MAV} \mid D) \le \gamma_2\). This is the same criterion with \(\gamma_{nogo} = 1 - \gamma_2\), so a threshold \(\gamma_2 = 0.2\) of the article corresponds to gamma_nogo = 0.8.

When smaller effects are favorable, as for the rate of an adverse event or a hazard ratio, direction = "less" in the criterion reverses the inequalities: the Go criterion is \(\Pr(\theta < \theta_{TV} \mid D) \ge \gamma_{go}\) and the NoGo criterion is \(\Pr(\theta \ge \theta_{MAV} \mid D) \ge \gamma_{nogo}\), with \(\theta_{TV} < \theta_{MAV}\) (see vignette("single-binary") and vignette("time-to-event")).

The criteria can instead be based on the posterior predictive probability of the effect \(\tilde\theta\) that will be observed in a future study with \(m_t\) and \(m_c\) subjects. With a null value \(\theta_{NULL}\), the Go criterion is \(\Pr(\tilde\theta > \theta_{NULL} \mid D) \ge \gamma_{go}\) and the NoGo criterion is \(\Pr(\tilde\theta \le \theta_{NULL} \mid D) \ge \gamma_{nogo}\).

For two endpoints, the target values and minimum acceptable values divide the plane of \((\theta_1, \theta_2)\) into nine regions, and the criteria are based on the probabilities of unions of regions or on each endpoint separately (see vignette("two-binary") and vignette("two-continuous")).

Specifying a study

A study is described by small specification objects, which are combined into a design.

Function Specifies
prior_beta(), prior_nix(), prior_dirichlet(), prior_niw(), prior_gamma(), prior_vague() Prior distribution of one group
data_binary(), data_continuous(), data_binary2(), data_continuous2(), data_tte() Summary of the data of one group
borrow() External data and the weight \(a_0\) of the power prior
hypothetical() Hypothetical control for a study without a concurrent control group
criterion_posterior(), criterion_predictive() Decision criterion
conduct_tte() Enrollment, dropout and time of the analysis of a study with a time-to-event endpoint
design_binary(), design_continuous(), design_binary2(), design_continuous2(), design_tte() Design of the study

The control group of a design takes one of four forms: a concurrent control group (n_c of at least 1 for binary and time-to-event endpoints, 2 for one continuous endpoint and 3 for two continuous endpoints), external control data only (n_c = 0 and external_c), a hypothetical control (n_c = 0 and hypothetical_c), or a fixed control (n_c = 0 and fixed_c), a known value of the control parameter as in a single-arm study compared with a benchmark. External data can also be borrowed for the treatment group (external_t).

Four functions work with the designs.

Function Computes
qdm_decide() The probabilities of the Go and NoGo regions and the decision for observed data
qdm_oc() The operating characteristics: the probabilities of Go, NoGo, Gray and Miss decisions under scenarios for the true parameters
qdm_calibrate() The thresholds \(\gamma_{go}\) and \(\gamma_{nogo}\) that control the probabilities of a false Go and a false NoGo decision
qdm_boundary() The observed data that lead to each decision, for example the numbers of responders, with the observed rates and effects (not available for two binary endpoints)

A first example

Consider a PoC study with one binary endpoint, 12 subjects in each group and Jeffreys priors \(\textrm{Beta}(0.5, 0.5)\). The team chooses \(\theta_{TV} = 0.2\), \(\theta_{MAV} = 0.1\), \(\gamma_{go} = 0.5\) and \(\gamma_{nogo} = 0.8\).

des <- design_binary(
  n_t = 12, n_c = 12,
  prior_t = prior_beta(0.5, 0.5), prior_c = prior_beta(0.5, 0.5),
  criterion = criterion_posterior(theta_tv = 0.2, theta_mav = 0.1,
                                  gamma_go = 0.5, gamma_nogo = 0.8)
)
des
#> Design: one binary endpoint
#>   Treatment: n = 12, prior Beta(a = 0.5, b = 0.5)
#>   Control: concurrent, n = 12, prior Beta(a = 0.5, b = 0.5)
#> Posterior probability criterion
#>   Go:   P(theta > 0.2 | data) >= 0.5
#>   NoGo: P(theta <= 0.1 | data) >= 0.8

If 9 of 12 subjects respond in the treatment group and 4 of 12 in the control group, the decision is:

dec <- qdm_decide(des, data_t = data_binary(12, 9),
                  data_c = data_binary(12, 4))
dec
#> Decision: Go
#>   Go:   P(theta > 0.2 | data) = 0.8517 (threshold 0.5, met)
#>   NoGo: P(theta <= 0.1 | data) = 0.05935 (threshold 0.8, not met)
#> Posterior distributions
#>   Treatment: Beta(a = 9.5, b = 3.5)
#>   Control:   Beta(a = 4.5, b = 8.5)

The posterior probability that the effect exceeds the target value is 0.852, and the decision is Go.

When the treatment has no effect (\(\pi_t = \pi_c = 0.3\)), the probability of a Go decision is 0.132. The thresholds can be calibrated so that the probability of meeting the Go criterion is at most 0.05 under no effect, and the probability of meeting the NoGo criterion is at most 0.2 when the effect is 0.3:

cal <- qdm_calibrate(des,
                     scenario_go = data.frame(pi_t = 0.3, pi_c = 0.3),
                     scenario_nogo = data.frame(pi_t = 0.6, pi_c = 0.3),
                     target_go = 0.05, target_nogo = 0.2)
cal

#>   gamma_nogo = 0.39: P(NoGo criterion met | scenario_nogo) = 0.182 (target 0.2)

The calibrated thresholds are \(\gamma_{go} =\) 0.74 and \(\gamma_{nogo} =\) 0.39, and cal$design is the design with these thresholds, which can be passed to qdm_decide() and qdm_oc().

Plots

The plot() methods return ggplot2 objects. Depending on the plot, their arguments set the colors of the decisions (col_go, col_nogo, col_gray and col_miss), the width of the lines (linewidth), the size of the points (point_size), the size of the text drawn in the plot (label_size), the base font size (base_size) and the position of the legend (legend_position); the help pages of plot.qdm_oc(), plot.qdm_calibration() and plot.qdm_boundary() list them. Anything else can be changed by adding ggplot2 functions, as for any other ggplot2 plot:

plot(oc, col_go = "darkgreen", col_nogo = "firebrick", linewidth = 0.7,
     base_size = 13, legend_position = "right") +
  ggplot2::labs(title = "Operating characteristics of the design")

Endpoints and computation

Endpoints Design Model Probabilities of the regions Operating characteristics
One binary design_binary() Beta-binomial Numerical integration (posterior), exact sum (predictive) Exact enumeration
One continuous design_continuous() Normal-Inverse-chi-squared or vague Numerical integration ("NI"), moment matching ("MM") or Monte Carlo ("MC") Simulation of nsim studies
Two binary design_binary2() Dirichlet-multinomial Monte Carlo with nMC draws (posterior), exact sum (predictive) Exact enumeration or simulation
Two continuous design_continuous2() Normal-Inverse-Wishart or vague Moment matching ("MM") or Monte Carlo ("MC") Simulation of nsim studies
One time-to-event design_tte() Exponential with Gamma prior (milestone survival or hazard ratio) Numerical integration or F distribution (posterior), simulation of nMC future studies (predictive) Simulation of nsim studies

The posterior distributions have closed forms, including those with external data borrowed through a power prior, so Markov chain Monte Carlo sampling is not needed. With a fixed control, the probabilities depend only on the posterior distribution of the treatment group and are computed from closed forms or by numerical integration, except for the posterior probabilities of two binary endpoints and the predictive probabilities of a time-to-event endpoint, which are Monte Carlo estimates. For one continuous endpoint, numerical integration is exact up to a small numerical error; the moment-matching approximation is faster by about two orders of magnitude and is suited to exploring designs, and the chosen design can then be checked with numerical integration. For two continuous endpoints, the moment-matching approximation can be checked with Monte Carlo estimates. The computing time of Monte Carlo estimates is proportional to the number of draws, and that of simulated operating characteristics to nsim.

Random numbers

qdm_decide(), qdm_oc(), qdm_calibrate() and qdm_boundary() have a seed argument for the random numbers they use (simulated studies and Monte Carlo estimates of probabilities). With seed = NULL (the default) they use the current random number stream, so set.seed() makes the results reproducible. With a given seed, the computation uses that seed and the previous state of the random number generator is restored on exit. ptdiff() with method = "MC" and rdirichlet() always use the current random number stream.

Validation

The scripts in the folder validation of the installed package compare BayesianQDM with published and submitted results (Kang et al., 2026; Huang, Talukder, Han and Kuan, 2019; Huang et al., submitted; Yamaguchi, Homma, Takeda and Maruo, submitted; Yamaguchi et al., submitted). The file results/summary.md in that folder reports the comparisons, with a registered reason for every difference beyond rounding:

system.file("validation", "results", "summary.md", package = "BayesianQDM")
