Single continuous endpoint

This vignette describes the analysis of a proof-of-concept (PoC) study with one normally distributed endpoint. vignette("overview") introduces the decision criteria.

Model

In group \(k\) (\(k = t, c\)), the outcomes follow \(N(\mu_k, \sigma_k^2)\), and the data are summarized by the number of subjects \(n_k\), the sample mean \(\bar y_k\) and the sample standard deviation \(s_k\) (data_continuous()). The treatment effect is \(\theta = \mu_t - \mu_c\).

With the Normal-Inverse-chi-squared prior (prior_nix()), \[\mu_k \mid \sigma_k^2 \sim N(\mu_0, \sigma_k^2 / \kappa_0), \qquad \sigma_k^2 \sim \textrm{Inv-}\chi^2(\nu_0, \sigma_0^2),\] the posterior distribution has the same form with parameters \(\kappa_n = \kappa_0 + n\), \(\mu_n = (\kappa_0 \mu_0 + n \bar y) / \kappa_n\), \(\nu_n = \nu_0 + n\) and \(\nu_n \sigma_n^2 = \nu_0 \sigma_0^2 + (n - 1) s^2 + \kappa_0 n (\bar y - \mu_0)^2 / \kappa_n\), and the posterior distribution of the mean is the t distribution \(t_{\nu_n}(\mu_n, \sigma_n^2 / \kappa_n)\). With the vague prior \(p(\mu, \sigma^2) \propto \sigma^{-2}\) (prior_vague()), it is \(t_{n - 1}(\bar y, s^2 / n)\).

For the predictive criterion, the mean of \(m\) future subjects of a group has the posterior predictive distribution \[t_{\nu_n}\left(\mu_n, \; \sigma_n^2 \left(\frac{1}{\kappa_n} + \frac{1}{m}\right)\right).\] Version 0.1.0 of the package used the squared scale \(\sigma_n^2 (1 + 1 / \kappa_n) / m\), which treats the \(m\) future subjects as independent draws from the predictive distribution of one subject and understates the uncertainty.

The posterior and predictive probabilities of the Go and NoGo regions are probabilities of the difference of two t variables, computed by ptdiff() with one of three methods (argument method of design_continuous()):

  • "NI" (the default): numerical integration over the whole real line, exact up to a small numerical error;
  • "MM": the moment-matching approximation of the difference by a single t distribution (Yamaguchi, Homma, Takeda and Maruo, submitted), which requires more than 4 degrees of freedom in both groups;
  • "MC": Monte Carlo simulation with nMC draws.

Decision for observed data

A study with 12 subjects in each group and vague priors, with \(\theta_{TV} = 15\) and \(\theta_{MAV} = 5\):

crit <- criterion_posterior(theta_tv = 15, theta_mav = 5,
                            gamma_go = 0.6, gamma_nogo = 0.7)
des <- design_continuous(n_t = 12, n_c = 12, prior_t = prior_vague(),
                         prior_c = prior_vague(), criterion = crit)
dec <- qdm_decide(des, data_t = data_continuous(n = 12, mean = 22, sd = 25),
                  data_c = data_continuous(n = 12, mean = 2, sd = 25))
dec
#> Decision: Go
#>   Go:   P(theta > 15 | data) = 0.6778 (threshold 0.6, met)
#>   NoGo: P(theta <= 5 | data) = 0.08784 (threshold 0.7, not met)
#> Posterior distributions
#>   Treatment: mean ~ t(df = 11, location = 22, scale = 7.217)
#>   Control:   mean ~ t(df = 11, location = 2, scale = 7.217)

The posterior distribution of each mean is \(t_{11}(\bar y, s^2 / 12)\). The three methods of ptdiff() give similar probabilities of the Go region:

post_t <- dec$posterior_t
post_c <- dec$posterior_c
scale_t <- post_t[["sigma"]] / sqrt(post_t[["kappa"]])
scale_c <- post_c[["sigma"]] / sqrt(post_c[["kappa"]])
set.seed(1)
sapply(c("NI", "MM", "MC"), function(m) {
  ptdiff(15, mu_t = post_t[["mu"]], scale_t = scale_t, df_t = post_t[["nu"]],
         mu_c = post_c[["mu"]], scale_c = scale_c, df_c = post_c[["nu"]],
         method = m, lower.tail = FALSE)
})
#>        NI        MM        MC 
#> 0.6777935 0.6780038 0.6791000

Prior distributions and external data

An informative Normal-Inverse-chi-squared prior is given by its parameters; for example, a control prior centered at 0 with the weight of 2 subjects for the mean and 4 degrees of freedom for the variance:

des_nix <- design_continuous(
  n_t = 12, n_c = 12, prior_t = prior_vague(),
  prior_c = prior_nix(mu0 = 0, kappa0 = 2, nu0 = 4, sigma0 = 25),
  criterion = crit
)
qdm_decide(des_nix, data_t = data_continuous(12, 22, 25),
           data_c = data_continuous(12, 2, 25))$posterior_c
#>        mu     kappa        nu     sigma 
#>  1.714286 14.000000 16.000000 24.214997

External data enter through a power prior with weight \(a_0\): data with \(n_e\) subjects, mean \(\bar y_e\) and standard deviation \(s_e\) act as \(a_0 n_e\) subjects with the same mean and the sum of squares \(a_0 (n_e - 1) s_e^2\), which keeps the posterior distribution in closed form (Huang et al., submitted). With the vague prior, the posterior distribution of the mean then has \(a_0 n_e + n - 1\) degrees of freedom. Borrowing half of 30 external control subjects:

des_ext <- design_continuous(
  n_t = 12, n_c = 12, prior_t = prior_vague(), prior_c = prior_vague(),
  criterion = crit,
  external_c = borrow(data_continuous(n = 30, mean = 0, sd = 25), a0 = 0.5)
)
dec_ext <- qdm_decide(des_ext, data_t = data_continuous(12, 22, 25),
                      data_c = data_continuous(12, 2, 25))
dec_ext$posterior_c
#>         mu      kappa         nu      sigma 
#>  0.8888889 27.0000000 26.0000000 24.7791527

The control mean now has 26 degrees of freedom (\(0.5 \times 30 + 12 - 1\)), and the probability of the Go region is 0.75 (without the external data, 0.678).

Hypothetical control

For an uncontrolled study, Kang, Yamaguchi and Han (2026, Section 4.2.4) give the control mean the same t distribution as the posterior distribution of the treatment mean, with location \(\mu_{0c}\) and the squared scale multiplied by \(r\): \[\mu_c \sim t_{\nu_{nt}}(\mu_{0c}, \; r \sigma_{nt}^2 / \kappa_{nt}).\] The prior of the control group is then not used and is left NULL:

des_hyp <- design_continuous(
  n_t = 12, n_c = 0, prior_t = prior_vague(), criterion = crit,
  hypothetical_c = hypothetical(mean = 0, r = 1)
)
qdm_decide(des_hyp, data_t = data_continuous(12, 22, 25))
#> Decision: Go
#>   Go:   P(theta > 15 | data) = 0.7403 (threshold 0.6, met)
#>   NoGo: P(theta <= 5 | data) = 0.06372 (threshold 0.7, not met)
#> Posterior distributions
#>   Treatment: mean ~ t(df = 11, location = 22, scale = 7.217)
#>   Control:   mean ~ t(df = 11, location = 0, scale = 7.217)

Fixed control

With fixed_c, the control mean is a known value \(\mu_{c0}\), for example the mean of the standard of care reported in the literature. The effect is \(\theta = \mu_t - \mu_{c0}\), its probabilities are those of the t distribution of the treatment mean, and prior_c and method are not used. With the vague prior and a benchmark of \(-10\):

des_fix <- design_continuous(n_t = 12, n_c = 0, prior_t = prior_vague(),
                             criterion = crit, fixed_c = -10)
dec_fix <- qdm_decide(des_fix, data_t = data_continuous(12, 22, 25))
dec_fix
#> Decision: Go
#>   Go:   P(theta > 15 | data) = 0.9809 (threshold 0.6, met)
#>   NoGo: P(theta <= 5 | data) = 0.00163 (threshold 0.7, not met)
#> Posterior distributions
#>   Treatment: mean ~ t(df = 11, location = 22, scale = 7.217)
#>   Control:   fixed value -10
pt((22 - (-10 + 15)) / (25 / sqrt(12)), df = 11)
#> [1] 0.9809453

For the predictive criterion, the mean of the \(m_t\) future subjects has the predictive t distribution given above, and the future effect is that mean minus \(\mu_{c0}\).

Predictive criterion

With 50 subjects per group in a future study and \(\theta_{NULL} = 0\):

des_pred <- design_continuous(
  n_t = 12, n_c = 12, prior_t = prior_vague(), prior_c = prior_vague(),
  criterion = criterion_predictive(theta_null = 0, m_t = 50,
                                   gamma_go = 0.9, gamma_nogo = 0.7)
)
qdm_decide(des_pred, data_t = data_continuous(12, 22, 25),
           data_c = data_continuous(12, 2, 25))
#> Decision: Go

Operating characteristics

qdm_oc() simulates nsim studies under each scenario. Only the sufficient statistics are simulated, \(\bar y = \mu + \sigma Z / \sqrt{n}\) and \(s = \sigma \sqrt{X / (n - 1)}\) with \(Z \sim N(0, 1)\) and \(X \sim \chi^2_{n - 1}\), and the same random numbers are used for all scenarios. The moment-matching approximation is much faster than numerical integration and is convenient for exploring designs:

des_mm <- design_continuous(n_t = 12, n_c = 12, prior_t = prior_vague(),
                            prior_c = prior_vague(), criterion = crit,
                            method = "MM")
scen <- data.frame(mu_t = seq(0, 40, by = 5), sd_t = 25, mu_c = 0, sd_c = 25)
oc <- qdm_oc(des_mm, scen, seed = 1)
oc
#> Operating characteristics (simulation of 10000 studies; probabilities by moment-matching approximation)
#>  mu_t sd_t mu_c sd_c     Go   NoGo   Gray Miss
#>     0   25    0   25 0.0419 0.4823 0.4758    0
#>     5   25    0   25 0.1087 0.2928 0.5985    0
#>    10   25    0   25 0.2264 0.1533 0.6203    0
#>    15   25    0   25 0.3876 0.0682 0.5442    0
#>    20   25    0   25 0.5825 0.0238 0.3937    0
#>    25   25    0   25 0.7631 0.0069 0.2300    0
#>    30   25    0   25 0.8817 0.0014 0.1169    0
#>    35   25    0   25 0.9508 0.0003 0.0489    0
#>    40   25    0   25 0.9840 0.0001 0.0159    0
plot(oc)

The chosen design can be checked with numerical integration. To keep this vignette quick, only three scenarios and 1000 simulated studies are used here; with the same seed, both methods are applied to the same simulated studies:

scen3 <- scen[c(1, 3, 5), ]
oc_ni <- qdm_oc(des, scen3, nsim = 1000, seed = 1)
oc_mm <- qdm_oc(des_mm, scen3, nsim = 1000, seed = 1)
data.frame(mu_t = scen3$mu_t, Go_NI = oc_ni$Go, Go_MM = oc_mm$Go,
           NoGo_NI = oc_ni$NoGo, NoGo_MM = oc_mm$NoGo)
#>   mu_t Go_NI Go_MM NoGo_NI NoGo_MM
#> 1    0 0.043 0.043   0.498   0.498
#> 2   10 0.209 0.209   0.180   0.180
#> 3   20 0.575 0.576   0.027   0.027

The simulation standard error of the probabilities is at most \(0.5 / \sqrt{nsim}\), 0.005 for the default nsim = 10000.

Decision boundary

qdm_boundary() shows which observed means lead to each decision. For each observed standard deviation (or sample size) of the treatment group, it gives the intervals of the observed treatment mean that lead to Go, NoGo and Gray decisions, and the corresponding observed differences in means; the ends of the intervals are located by bisection. With a concurrent control group, the observed control data are given by data_c:

b <- qdm_boundary(des_mm, sd_t = seq(10, 40, by = 5),
                  data_c = data_continuous(12, 2, 25))

Calibration of the thresholds

The thresholds are calibrated so that the Go criterion is met with probability at most 0.05 when the treatment has no effect, and the NoGo criterion with probability at most 0.2 when the effect is 20:

cal <- qdm_calibrate(
  des_mm,
  scenario_go = data.frame(mu_t = 0, sd_t = 25, mu_c = 0, sd_c = 25),
  scenario_nogo = data.frame(mu_t = 20, sd_t = 25, mu_c = 0, sd_c = 25),
  target_go = 0.05, target_nogo = 0.2, seed = 1
)
cal
#> Calibration of the decision thresholds (simulation of 10000 studies; probabilities by moment-matching approximation)
#>   gamma_go   = 0.57: P(Go criterion met | scenario_go) = 0.0484 (target 0.05)
#>   gamma_nogo = 0.28: P(NoGo criterion met | scenario_nogo) = 0.1975 (target 0.2)
#> Decision probabilities with the chosen thresholds
#>       scenario mu_t sd_t mu_c sd_c     Go   NoGo   Gray Miss
#>    scenario_go    0   25    0   25 0.0484 0.8615 0.0901    0
#>  scenario_nogo   20   25    0   25 0.6135 0.1975 0.1890    0
plot(cal)

The chosen thresholds are \(\gamma_{go} =\) 0.57 and \(\gamma_{nogo} =\) 0.28.
